Services

What we make,

Four groups of work. Each one lists what we need from you before it can be quoted properly, because that is usually what decides whether the date holds.

01 — Signs

Storefront &
illuminated

The sign a customer sees from the road, and the ones that keep the inside of the building legible.

We make

  • Storefront signs
  • Illuminated signs
  • Channel letters
  • Storefront wraps and window graphics
  • Interior, directional and wayfinding signage

To quote it we need

  • Site address and a photo of the wall or fascia
  • Approximate width and height available
  • Mounting surface and whether power is at the location
  • Landlord or corporate sign criteria, if there are any
  • Your artwork, or whatever version of it exists

Illuminated and permitted work involves a municipality and often a landlord. We prepare and coordinate what we can; the approval is theirs to give, and the requirements are not the same in every city.

02 — Vehicles

Wraps &
fleet graphics

One work van, a service fleet on a rotation, or trade printing for installers who need a reliable print supplier.

We make

  • Vehicle graphics and lettering
  • Partial and full vehicle wraps
  • Fleet and commercial rollouts
  • Trade printing for freelance installers
  • Repeat vehicles built to a saved spec

To quote it we need

  • Year, make, model and body style
  • Number of vehicles and whether they arrive together
  • Coverage: lettering, partial, or full wrap
  • Where the vehicles can be left, and for how long
  • Artwork, or the brand files you already use

Fleet work is the kind of job we want more of. If vehicles come in on a schedule, the specification stays on file so number seven matches number one without rebuilding the artwork.

03 — Print

Banners &
commercial print

The recurring, deadline-driven work: campaigns, seasonal menus, events, product labels.

We make

  • Banners
  • Backdrops and step-and-repeat
  • Vinyl printing and cut vinyl
  • Labels and decals
  • Magnetics

To quote it we need

  • Finished size, in inches or feet
  • Quantity
  • Indoor or outdoor, and how long it needs to last
  • Finishing: hems, grommets, pole pockets, cut lines
  • The date it has to be in your hands

Print files get opened and checked before they are run. If the resolution will not hold at size, or a colour build is going to shift on the material, you hear about it first.

04 — Regulated signage

ADA &
fire signage

The signage an inspector, a tenant or a fire marshal expects to find in the building.

We make

  • ADA room and restroom signage
  • Tactile and braille signage
  • Fire and life-safety signage
  • Occupancy, exit and directional plates
  • Interior sign packages for a whole floor or suite

To quote it we need

  • A floor plan or a room-by-room list
  • Sign type and message per location
  • Any building standard or tenant manual you have to follow
  • Mounting surface at each door
  • Inspection or handover date

Requirements here are set by code and by the authority reviewing your building, not by us. Send the standard you are being held to and we will produce to it.

Scope and responsibility

What we own,
and what we
do not.

Most shops in this category advertise that they handle everything. Almost none of them own the permit decision, the landlord's criteria, or a licensed electrical tie-in. Here is how the work actually divides.

Ours

What Master Signs owns

  • File review and prepress on every job
  • Vinyl, banner, label and graphics production
  • Vehicle graphics and wrap printing
  • Specification, material call and production release
  • Scheduling, delivery, pickup and coordination
  • Acceptance, punch list and the reorder record

Shared or external

What sits with someone else

  • Some fabrication and illuminated work — trade partners
  • Licensed electrical connection — licensed trade
  • Permit and zoning decisions — the municipality
  • Sign criteria and brand approval — landlord or franchisor
  • Final artwork ownership and rights — you
  • Site access, power and operating hours — the property

When a trade partner is on your job, you are told before you commit. That is a deliberate policy: a vendor who hides subcontracting is a vendor who cannot tell you who to call when something needs fixing.
